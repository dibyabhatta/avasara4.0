Avasara Academy

Apply smarter.

Step 1

Check eligibility

Citizenship, degree level, GPA, language score and field. Rule out mismatches before you write anything.

Step 2

Contact supervisors

For PhD and research masters: 5–8 lines, specific research fit, your CV attached, one clear ask.

Step 3

Prove your English

Most universities accept IELTS, TOEFL or PTE. Some waive it for prior English-medium study; confirm on the official page.

Step 4

Write your statement

Why this field, what you have done, why this place, what you will do next. Specific beats impressive.

Step 5

Line up referees

Ask 4–6 weeks ahead. Send your CV, the program details and the deadline.

Know the terms

RTP vs HDR

In Australia, RTP is the government-funded place (fees and stipend). HDR means Higher Degree by Research (Masters by research or PhD). Most universities apply you for both at once.

USAPhD

PhD in the USA

Strong programs usually fund admitted PhD students with a stipend and tuition waiver, through assistantships or fellowships. You apply to the program, not to a separate scholarship. Ask about funding before you accept.

USAMaster’s

Master’s in the USA

Funding is limited and mostly partial: departmental awards, merit aid and some assistantships. Check each department’s funding page and the deadline for aid, which can be earlier than admission.

USABachelor

Bachelor’s in the USA

International aid comes from the university and is usually decided at admission. A few, such as Harvard, MIT, Princeton, Yale and Amherst, say they are need-blind for internationals. Confirm the policy each year.

A note from the founder

Why I started Avasara.

Avasara was born from a simple frustration: opportunities are everywhere, but finding them shouldn’t be so difficult.

I’m Dibya Bhatta, a third-year PhD student at the Australian National University (ANU). Having experienced scholarship-supported study in South Korea and now continuing my academic journey at ANU, I know how life-changing access to the right opportunity can be.

I often share scholarships and study opportunities on Facebook and LinkedIn. But useful information gets buried quickly in social media feeds. A scholarship posted today can be almost impossible to find tomorrow.

That’s why I started Avasara: one place to discover scholarships, explore study destinations and find practical guidance for applying.

My hope is that a student who has never heard of a particular country, university or funding opportunity can discover it here—and take a step towards a future they might not have thought possible.

Avasara means opportunity. And everyone deserves the chance to find theirs.

If you want personal guidance with your application, you can reach me here.
